Shifting Sands

A Game of Persian Glory, Court Intrigue, and the Relentless Greek Advance

Prologue: A Word from the Great King

“The Empire is vast, but its sands shift with every whisper, every coin, every blade drawn in the dark. You, noble satrap, must guide your province through war, politics, and treachery. Earn Honor in the eyes of the King, and your name shall be etched into the annals of Persia. Fail… and the Greeks will write your epitaph.”

Welcome to Shifting Sands, a competitive strategy game of worker placement, economic maneuvering, political manipulation, and battlefield brinkmanship. You and your fellow satraps serve the Persian Empire during the Greco-Persian conflicts. Each round, you will deploy your Leaders to take actions, build your province, influence the Court, and prepare for the inevitable clash with the Greeks.

Only one truth matters: the satrap with the most Honor when the Greek army is finally defeated will be remembered as the Empire’s greatest servant.

1. Components

1.1 Player Components (per player)

10 Leader dice (your potential Leaders)

4 starting Leaders (placed at level 2)

4 starting Investments (placed at level 2)

1 Player Shield

Favor tokens

Troops (public)

Spies (hidden)

Card markers for Schemes

1.2 Shared Components

Investment Deck (2 copies of each card)

Law Deck (2 copies of each card)

Scheme Deck (3 copies of each card)

Royal Bank

Market Rate die

Battlefield board

Greek Reserves

Scheme Envelopes

King’s Favor token

Margin Note — Persian Fact #1: The Persian Empire maintained a complex administrative system with satraps governing provinces. Many were loyal… many were not.

2. Objective

Your goal is simple to state and difficult to achieve:

Earn the most Honor by the time the Greek army is defeated.

Honor is gained through:

Winning Duels

Winning Battles

Deploying the most Troops

Holding the King’s Favor

Sponsoring Laws

Certain card effects

When the Greek reserves are empty during the Resolution Phase, the game ends immediately.

3. Setup

3.1 Global Setup

Place the Battlefield and Greek Reserves in the center.

Place 4 Greeks per player into the Greek Reserves.

Place 3 Coins + 1 per player into the Royal Bank.

Set the Market Rate die to 3.

Shuffle the Investment, Law, and Scheme decks.

Deal each player 2 cards from each deck.

Give each player 4 Troops and 1 Spy.

Give each player a Scheme envelope containing 2 markers for each player.

Give the King’s Favor to the Veteran (only during training games).

3.2 Player Setup

Place your 4 starting Leaders in front of your shield at level 2.

Place your 4 starting Investments in front of your shield at level 2.

Place one Favor token on the score track.

Keep Coins, Commodities, and Spies behind your shield.

Margin Note — Persian Fact #2: Persian nobles often kept their true wealth hidden. Coins behind the shield are historically accurate.

4. Game Structure

Each round has two major phases:

A. Action Phase

B. Resolution Phase (formerly “Night Phase”)

The King’s Favor determines who begins each round.

5. Action Phase

Players take turns clockwise. On your turn:

Place one unused Leader on an available action space and take that action.

5.1 Leader Levels

A Leader’s level determines:

How many resources they gain

How many cards they draw

How many Troops/Spies they recruit

How many Coins they can trade

How many Laws they can influence

5.2 Action Space Types

There are three categories of actions:

A. Court Actions

Only one player may use each Court space per round.

These represent influence at the royal court.

B. Provincial Actions

Each player may use each Provincial space once.

These represent governing your province.

C. Undermarket Actions

Each player may use each Undermarket space once.

These represent illicit dealings outside the King’s gaze.

5.3 Passing

Once you have used all your Leaders, you must pass for the rest of the round.

Margin Note — Persian Fact #3: The Persian court was notorious for its bureaucracy. Only one satrap could “petition” the King at a time.

6. Actions Overview

Below is a concise summary. The full rulebook will include a detailed appendix.

Provincial Actions

Recruit: Gain Troops (pay 1 Commodity each)

Harvest: Gain Commodities

Build: Create a new Investment

Upgrade: Increase an Investment’s level

Commission: Gain a new Leader

Promote: Increase a Leader’s level

Brainstorm: Draw cards

Negotiate: Exchange Favors with another player

Duel: Challenge another player’s Leader

Court Actions

Flatter: Gain the King’s Favor and Coins

Prepare: Peek at Laws

Petition: Add Laws to the queue

Postpone: Move the deliberated Law back

Buy/Sell: Trade with the Royal Bank

Monetize: Convert cards into Coins

Undermarket Actions

Buy/Sell: Trade outside the Royal Bank

Monetize: Convert cards into Coins at a different rate

7. Laws

Laws shape the Empire. They are:

Proposed

Deliberated

Ratified

Enforced

Each Law has a Value Die representing its Threshold (formerly “boundary”).

7.1 Thresholds

A Law’s threshold determines whether a player is obeying or violating the Law.

7.2 Sanctions

If you violate an active Law:

When you take the sanctioned action, your Leader acts at one level lower.

You will owe a Tax during the Resolution Phase.

7.3 Modifying Laws

Some Law cards allow players to:

Select an existing active Law

Replace its sponsor

Set a new threshold

These cards are discarded after use.

Margin Note — Persian Fact #4: Persian kings frequently rewrote laws to favor loyal satraps. History repeats itself.

8. Schemes

Schemes represent covert operations, sabotage, bribery, and assassination.

8.1 Submitting Schemes

At any time during the Action Phase, you may:

Choose a Scheme card from your hand.

Attach two markers:

Top-left: Target player

Bottom-right: Receiving player

Place 1 Spy into the envelope for each Scheme submitted.

Insert the Scheme into your envelope.

You may submit any number of Schemes.

8.2 Resolving Schemes

During the Resolution Phase:

All envelopes are collected and shuffled.

Invalid Schemes (missing markers or Spy) are discarded.

For each valid Scheme:

Target rolls a d6.

If roll > half their Troops → Scheme succeeds.

Otherwise → Scheme fails.

If the Scheme has a $D6, the target rolls again to determine the effect.

All Schemes and Spies used are discarded.

Margin Note — Persian Fact #5: The Persian Empire employed an elite spy network known as The King’s Eyes and Ears. Your spies are… less elite.

9. Battles

Battles occur every round during the Resolution Phase.

9.1 Determining the General

The player who deployed the most Troops becomes the General and gains 1 Honor.

9.2 Determining Greek Forces

Roll 1 die per player, halve each (round up), and sum the results. This is the number of Greek attackers.

9.3 Battle Resolution

Compare:

Total Persian Troops deployed

Total Greek attackers

If Persians Win:

Each player who deployed Troops gains 1 Honor.

Remaining Persians kill an equal number of Greek reserves.

If Greeks Win:

All Persian Troops are removed.

Equal number of Greek attackers are removed.

Remaining Greeks attack the player who deployed the fewest Troops.

That player must:

Remove Troops equal to the Greek count, or

If insufficient, downgrade Investments using the Greek dice rolls.

9.4 Downgrading Investments

If forced to downgrade:

Reduce the Investment to level 1.

If already level 1 → discard it.

This is identical to Scheme-based destruction.

Margin Note — Persian Fact #6: Greek hoplites were famously disciplined. Persian satraps… less so.

10. Resolution Phase

This phase cleans up the round and advances the Empire.

10.1 Taxes

Players violating Laws must pay Coins to the Royal Bank.

10.2 Battle

Resolve the Greek attack.

10.3 Schemes

Resolve all Schemes.

10.4 Law Ratification

The deliberated Law becomes active.

10.5 Market Rate Adjustment

Roll a die:

Higher → Market Rate +1

Lower → Market Rate -1

Equal → No change

10.6 Check for Game End

If the Greek reserves are empty → Game ends immediately.

10.7 Reset

Return Leaders to players

Reset envelopes

Reveal next deliberated Law

11. End of the Game

When the Greek reserves are empty, the game ends.

11.1 Final Scoring

Players total their Honor from:

Duels

Battles

King’s Favor

Sponsored Laws

Investments and cards that grant Honor

11.2 Winner

The player with the most Honor is declared:

Grand Satrap of the Persian Empire

“Your name shall be carved into the stone of Persepolis. The sands shift no more.”
